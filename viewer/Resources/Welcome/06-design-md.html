Design Research Tokens, screenshots, notes, references

Turn design-system research into a local collection.

A Design.md collection is a folder for studying the visual language of a website or product. It can hold extracted colors, typography, spacing, screenshots, token files, and your own design notes.

What to put in DESIGN.md

SectionPurpose
SourceThe public URL or product area you studied.
Color NotesPrimary, secondary, accent, background, and neutral colors.
TypographyDetected fonts, heading scale, body size, weight, and rhythm.
LayoutSpacing scale, container width, grids, and page density.
ComponentsObserved buttons, cards, forms, navigation, badges, and empty states.
Use With CareWhat is inspiration, what is extracted fact, and what still needs review.

Extraction workflow

  1. Pick a public website. Use a page that represents the visual style you want to study.
  2. Extract primitives. Capture likely colors, fonts, spacing, radius, shadows, and starter token files.
  3. Review the output. Treat extraction as initialization, not a complete design system.
  4. Save the artifacts locally. Keep raw output, normalized output, token files, screenshots, and notes in one folder.
  5. Open the folder in HTTMELY. Browse it like a small design reference library.

The goal is not pixel-perfect copying. The goal is a portable research packet that helps you understand a visual system before you design your own.

Use with an AI assistant

A design extraction assistant can create the first draft: DESIGN.md, tokens.css, tokens.json, screenshot notes, and component observations. Then you review it, correct it, and open the folder in HTTMELY as a calm local reference.

If your release bundle includes the HTTMELY collection design skill, pair it with a design-system extraction workflow: extraction gathers raw visual facts, and the collection design step turns those facts into a readable local library.
